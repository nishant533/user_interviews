The AI Adoption Paradox
Ideation & Concept Brief

Focused on Pain Points 1 & 2 — role-specificity gap and the decomposition wall · May 2026

Pain Point 1
No role-specific reference point
Professionals can't start using AI meaningfully because they've never seen someone in their exact role do it.
Pain Point 2
The decomposition wall
Professionals know they want to automate something but can't name the trigger, steps, or output — so they never start.

Three buckets of opportunity

Ideas are separated by which pain point they address — and where they address both at once.

Solves Pain Point 1 only Role visibility — showing people what's possible in their job

RoleRadar

HMW show a user what AI looks like for their exact job — without asking them to search for it?
HMW replace the generic "AI for PMs" YouTube video with a structured, ranked list of what PMs like Surbhi actually do?
HMW make role-specific AI use cases discoverable the way LinkedIn shows you jobs — based on who you are, not what you search?
Wild idea You enter your job title and company type. The product surfaces the top 7 AI use cases for your role, ranked by how many similar professionals have used them — each with a one-paragraph "here's exactly how it works for me."
→ RoleRadar

ShadowShift

HMW give someone a live, unedited view of a same-role professional's AI-assisted workday?
HMW make peer proof accessible without requiring a personal connection?
HMW create a "job shadow" experience but for AI usage — 20 minutes with someone in your exact role showing their actual screen?
Wild idea A structured "shadow session" — 20 mins, same job title, one person shows three real AI moments from their week. Not a tutorial. Not a course. Just a screen share of real work.
→ ShadowShift

RoleProof

HMW build a crowdsourced library of short "this is how I use AI for my job" clips — searchable by role and task, not by tool?
HMW replace aspirational influencer content with authentic, unglamorous proof from real practitioners?
HMW make it as easy to find "AI for ops analyst" as it is to find a recipe?
Wild idea A searchable library of 2-minute screen recordings tagged by role, company size, and task type. Not polished — raw, real, relatable. "Here's what changed for me on a Tuesday."
→ RoleProof

WorkBreaker

HMW translate plain-English work frustrations into structured automation specs — without requiring the user to know what a "trigger" is?
HMW replace "what do you want to automate?" with "what's the thing you dread doing every Monday?"
HMW make decomposition a conversation, not a form?
Wild idea A 5-message conversation: "Describe your most annoying recurring task in plain English." AI asks 3 follow-up questions, then outputs a complete trigger-steps-output spec ready to hand to any automation tool.
→ WorkBreaker

TaskScan

HMW surface automation candidates by reading what someone already has — their JD, meeting notes, or inbox — rather than asking them to recall it?
HMW give someone a pre-built shortlist of what to automate before they've thought about it?
HMW lower the cognitive load of "what should I build?" to near zero?
Wild idea Paste your job description or last week's meeting notes. TaskScan identifies 3-5 automation candidates and writes the spec for each. No form, no questions — just paste and receive.
→ TaskScan
Solves both Pain Points 1 + 2 Role proof + decomposition — see it in your role, then spec it for your work

RoleSpec

HMW show someone what people in their role have built AND hand them the spec to replicate it — in one step?
HMW turn peer proof into a directly executable artifact, not just inspiration?
HMW collapse "I've never seen my role do this" and "I don't know how to describe it" into a single solved problem?
Wild idea A library of role-specific, pre-decomposed automation specs — built by real practitioners. You pick your role, browse 10 real workflows, tap one, and the full spec (trigger, steps, output, tools needed) is already written. You just adapt it to your context.
→ RoleSpec

PeerBuild

HMW pair someone with a same-role peer who doesn't just demo their workflow but helps the user map it to their own job?
HMW make peer proof productive — not just inspiring but directly translating into the user's first buildable spec?
HMW turn the activation moment into the decomposition moment simultaneously?
Wild idea A structured 30-min session: same-role peer shows ONE workflow (proof), then together they map it to the user's context (decomposition). Session ends with a filled spec the user owns. Facilitated by a template, not a mentor.
→ PeerBuild

FirstSpec

HMW give a user their first automation spec in under 10 minutes — grounded in what real people in their role have built?
HMW use role-specific examples as the input to a personal decomposition, not just inspiration?
HMW make "I've never done this" feel like "I'm just adapting something that already exists"?
Wild idea You enter your role and one sentence about your work. The product shows you the 3 closest workflows built by people like you, then asks "which of these is closest to your situation?" — and uses your answer to pre-fill your personal spec. Role proof leads, decomposition follows.
→ FirstSpec

Top 3 Concept Briefs

Each concept attacks a different layer of the same root problem

Concept 1 · Solves Both · Role Library + Decomposition
RoleSpec
A library of real automation specs built by people in your exact role. Pick one. Adapt it. Build it.
#1

The barriers it breaks

PP1: You've never seen someone in your role do this. PP2: You can't describe what you'd build. RoleSpec solves both simultaneously — you browse real specs from real practitioners in your role, pick the closest one, and the decomposition is already done for you. You adapt, not create from scratch.
"I've never seen a video that says here's how an ops analyst uses AI to do their job better."— Surbhi, Ops Analyst

Key features

  • Role onboarding: job title + company type (2 fields, 60 seconds)
  • Browse 10–15 pre-built specs from real practitioners in that role — each with: what it does, time saved per week, tools needed, difficulty rating
  • Every spec is structured: trigger → steps → output → edge cases
  • "Make it mine" flow: 3 questions that adapt the spec to your specific context
  • Export to Zapier / n8n / plain doc — one click
  • Practitioners contribute specs (earn visibility); specs are reviewed for quality before publishing

What it is not

Not a course. Not a tutorial. Not a prompt library. It is a role-specific, practitioner-built, structured automation spec library — the thing between "I want to automate something" and "I know exactly what to build."

Why existing products miss this

YouTube has tutorials but they're tool-centric, not role-centric. ADPList has peers but sessions are unstructured. Zapier has templates but they're not role-aware. No product gives you a pre-written spec grounded in what someone with your actual job title has built. The role-spec layer is unbuilt.
Segment

P2-new professionals (ops analysts, PMs, marketing managers, support leads) who are aware AI could help their job, have never seen it done for their role, and cannot describe what to build when asked. They need proof and a spec, not a course.

Concept 2 · Solves Both · Live Peer Activation + Spec
PeerBuild
A structured 30-min session: same-role peer shows one workflow, then helps you map it to your own job. You leave with a spec, not just inspiration.
#2

The barriers it breaks

PP1: The strongest finding in the research — same-role peer proof is the activation event, not content. PP2: The session doesn't end with inspiration, it ends with a filled decomposition spec. The peer proof moment and the decomposition moment are the same event — engineered, not accidental.
"I even need to know what questions to ask."— Saransh, Principal IC PM

Key features

  • Match by job title + org type (not industry alone — research confirmed this)
  • Structured session template: Part 1 (15 min) — peer shows one real workflow from their actual job. Part 2 (15 min) — guided mapping: "Which part of this applies to your work?"
  • Session ends with a shared doc: the peer's spec + the user's adapted version, side by side
  • Facilitator guide ensures Part 2 always produces a spec — not open-ended chat
  • Peers are rated on whether the attendee built something within 7 days
  • Async fallback: if no live peer available, access a recorded session from same role + org type with a guided adaptation worksheet

What it is not

Not mentorship. Not coaching. Not a Q&A session. It is a structured handoff event — the peer shows one thing, the user maps it to their context, and both leave with a spec. The relationship is the vehicle, not the product.

Why existing products miss this

ADPList offers peer sessions but they're unstructured — no template, no spec output, no follow-through mechanism. Every existing peer product ends when the session ends. PeerBuild ends when the user has a spec. That gap is structural and unaddressed by anything in the competitive landscape.
Segment

P2-new professionals who are aware of the gap, have tried (course, YouTube, tutorial) and stalled, and are most likely to convert when shown proof by someone in the same job. The matching must be by title + org-type — not industry alone, as Rohan's case confirms.

Concept 3 · Solves Pain Point 2 · Conversational Decomposition
WorkBreaker
You describe your most annoying Monday task in plain English. WorkBreaker translates it into a buildable spec — no technical knowledge required.
#3

The barrier it breaks

PP2 specifically: The decomposition wall exists because users are asked "what do you want to automate?" — a technically-framed question they can't answer. WorkBreaker reframes the entry point as "what's the most annoying thing you do repeatedly?" — a question anyone can answer — and does the decomposition for them through dialogue.
"What should I automate? I don't even know where to start."— Composite from 5 P3 interviews

Key features

  • Conversational interface — 5 exchanges maximum
  • Opens with: "Describe the most annoying recurring task in your week. Plain English, no jargon."
  • AI asks 3 focused follow-up questions: frequency, who else is involved, what the output looks like
  • Outputs a complete spec: trigger, inputs, steps, output, recommended tool
  • Spec is editable inline before export
  • "Not quite right" loop: user can flag any spec field and WorkBreaker re-asks that specific question
  • Saves a library of specs over time — users build a personal automation backlog

What it is not

Not an automation builder. Not a chatbot for productivity tips. Not a workflow tool. It is purely the decomposition layer — the step that currently happens nowhere, leaving users with intent and no spec. It hands off the output to any automation tool.

Why existing products miss this

Zapier and n8n assume users arrive with a spec. ChatGPT can help decompose but requires the user to know what to ask — the same problem. No product makes "describe your problem in plain English → receive buildable spec" its core job. WorkBreaker is the missing translation layer between intent and execution.
Segment

P2-new professionals who have intent and motivation but stall at the blank page of "what do I build?" They're not stuck for lack of tools — they're stuck because no product speaks to them in human language and hands them back a technical artifact. WorkBreaker is also the natural follow-on for users who complete a RoleSpec session and want to go further.

Open Questions for Phase 3

What needs to be validated before building

Critical — RoleSpec

Will practitioners actually contribute specs — and will they be honest about what they built?

RoleSpec's value depends entirely on the quality and authenticity of contributed specs. If practitioners only submit polished, aspirational workflows (not what they actually use), the product fails the same way YouTube AI influencer content does. Test: recruit 5 practitioners, ask them to write a spec for something they genuinely use weekly, and measure whether the output is specific enough to be buildable by someone else.

Critical — PeerBuild

Can the structured template force a spec output — or will Part 2 drift into open conversation?

The mechanism depends on Part 2 producing a filled spec, not just a good chat. If the facilitation template isn't tight enough, sessions end with inspiration and no artifact — the same failure mode as ADPList. Test: run 3 sessions with the template and measure whether every session ends with a spec that a third party can evaluate as "buildable without further clarification."

Critical — WorkBreaker

Does a conversational spec feel credible enough to act on — or does it feel like a ChatGPT output?

WorkBreaker's risk is the "AI generated this, it probably won't work" perception. The spec needs to feel grounded and specific — not generic. Test: show 5 P2-new users a WorkBreaker-generated spec vs a human-written spec for the same task. Measure whether they trust it enough to take it to an automation tool. If not, the product needs a "reviewed by a practitioner" layer.

High — RoleSpec + PeerBuild

Is role + org-type specific enough as a matching criterion — or do users need task-level matching?

Rohan confirmed that cross-role doesn't transfer. But Surbhi's frustration suggests even same-role content fails if the task context is different (startup ops vs enterprise ops). PeerBuild and RoleSpec both match by title + org-type. Test whether task-type specificity matters more than title specificity for activation — or if title + org-type is granular enough to produce the "that's exactly my situation" moment.

High — All three

Do these produce a crossing to P3-new — or do they just produce a single spec?

All three concepts are designed to produce a first automation spec. But the research also showed that daily AI usage can plateau without expansion. A single spec may not produce the identity shift ("I'm someone who builds with AI") needed for sustained behaviour change. Phase 3 needs to test whether completing one spec leads to a second — or whether the user returns to surface usage after one build.
